Provider Audit FAQs

What do telecom providers look for in audits?

Telecom provider audits typically examine sales practices, consent documentation, complaint handling and regulatory compliance. Auditors review sample contracts for evidence of informed customer consent, proper disclosures and contract adherence. They also check complaint rates, clawback history and any regulatory action against your operation.

How often do Virgin Media, O2 and Sky audit resellers?

Major providers conduct routine audits annually or bi-annually, with additional targeted audits triggered by complaint spikes, clawback rates or regulatory concerns. New resellers typically face audits within their first 6-12 months. High-volume resellers may be audited more frequently.

What happens if I fail a provider audit?

Failed audits can result in commission clawbacks, sales restrictions, remediation requirements or termination of your reseller agreement. The severity depends on the findings—documentation issues may require process improvements, while consent violations or mis-selling evidence can trigger immediate suspension.

How far in advance should I prepare for an audit?

Audit preparation should be ongoing, not reactive. Compliance documentation should be generated for every sale as it happens. However, if you receive audit notification, allow 2-4 weeks for document gathering, gap analysis and remediation of any issues before the review date.

What's the difference between a provider audit and an Ofcom investigation?

A provider audit is a commercial review carried out by the network or service provider (like Virgin Media, O2 or Sky) checking a reseller's own sales practices and paperwork, while an Ofcom investigation is a regulatory action under the General Conditions, usually triggered by wider complaint patterns or a formal referral. Passing provider audits consistently reduces the chance of ever reaching Ofcom's attention in the first place.

What percentage of my sales calls will an auditor actually review?

Auditors typically review a sample of your sales rather than every single one — industry-wide call quality audits commonly sample somewhere in the low single-digit percentage of total volume. Because only a fraction of calls are checked, having consistent, complete verification records across all sales matters more than hoping the ones reviewed happen to be clean.

Can I lose my reseller agreement completely after repeated audit failures?

Yes — providers can terminate a reseller agreement after a pattern of repeated audit failures, not just after a single incident. A history of failed audits, unresolved findings or rising complaint rates is what typically escalates from a warning to suspension to termination.

Does having verification records guarantee I'll pass a provider audit?

No — verification records are strong evidence of consent, but auditors also review complaint handling, contract documentation, agent training and clawback history. Verification covers the consent side of the audit thoroughly; it's one major piece of a broader compliance picture, not the whole picture.

What's the difference between a scheduled audit and an unannounced spot check?

A scheduled audit is planned in advance, giving you time to prepare documentation, while a spot check or mystery shop happens without warning to see how sales actually run day to day. Because spot checks can't be prepped for in the moment, the only real defence is having verification and documentation practices consistently in place, not switched on before a known audit date.

Is a One Touch Switch (OTS) compliance review the same as a standard provider sales audit?

No — a One Touch Switch review checks your switching process specifically (how gaining and losing provider handoffs are handled), while a standard provider sales audit looks more broadly at consent, contract terms and sales conduct. A reseller can be fully OTS-compliant and still fail a broader sales audit, or vice versa, since they check different things.

What happens to commission I've already been paid if an audit finds a problem with the sale?

If an audit uncovers a problem with a specific sale — such as unclear consent or a misrepresented term — the commission already paid on that sale is typically clawed back retroactively. This is separate from routine churn-based clawbacks; it's tied directly to the audit finding a fault with how the sale was made.

Why can't I just review my own sales calls instead of using an independent verification service?

You can review your own calls, but providers and regulators give self-reviewed records far less weight than independently verified ones, since the seller has an obvious interest in the outcome. Independent verification removes that conflict of interest, which is exactly what auditors are looking to confirm is in place.

What documents do auditors ask for beyond the verification recording itself?

Beyond the verification recording, auditors commonly ask for the signed or accepted contract, complaint logs, agent training records, and a history of clawback or cancellation rates. The verification recording proves consent at the point of sale; these other documents show the wider process around it was sound.

How long does a provider audit take from notification to final outcome?

Timelines vary by provider, but once notified you should expect a review period of several weeks covering document submission, sample review and any follow-up queries before a final outcome is issued. This is distinct from how far ahead you should prepare — ongoing readiness matters more than the length of the audit itself once it starts.
