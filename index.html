AI Agent Standards

The AI Agent Standards Your Business Needs to Know in 2026

AI assistants and shopping agents are starting to find, compare, book and pay for services on behalf of customers. A handful of open standards decide whether they can find and trust your business. Here is what each one does and how Heimdell Tech Ai helps.

A2A (Agent2Agent protocol)

An open standard, governed by the Linux Foundation, that lets AI agents from different vendors discover each other, exchange messages and hand over tasks. Version 1.0 arrived in March 2026. Your business publishes an Agent Card that says what you offer and how to book it. We build and test the Agent Card and the booking integration.

A2A protocol implementation →

AP2 (Agent Payments Protocol)

Google's open protocol for letting AI agents pay on a customer's behalf, using signed mandates that record exactly what the customer authorised. Google donated AP2 to the FIDO Alliance in April 2026. We help you accept agent payments with verifiable proof of every authorisation.

AP2 agent payments →

UCP (Universal Commerce Protocol)

A commerce standard that lets agents browse your catalogue, manage a cart and check out. Many Shopify stores already publish a UCP profile; other platforms have to set one up. We check your profile and your product data so agents can read both.

UCP readiness →

MCP (Model Context Protocol)

An open standard for connecting AI assistants to tools and data, such as your booking system, product database or knowledge base. We help you expose the right information safely, with permissions you control.

MCP servers →

Knowledge Catalog

Google Cloud's Knowledge Catalog, formerly Dataplex Universal Catalog, gives AI agents governed, searchable context about business data. The same principle applies to any business: clean, structured, accurate facts about your products, prices and policies. We prepare that foundation.

Frequently Asked Questions

AI Agent Readiness — Frequently Asked Questions

Straight answers about A2A, AP2, UCP, MCP and getting your business ready for AI agents.

What does it mean for a business to be AI agent-ready?

+
An AI agent-ready business is one that software acting for a customer, such as an AI assistant or shopping agent, can find, understand and transact with. That means accurate structured data about your products, services, prices and policies, a clear way to book or buy, and, increasingly, support for open standards such as A2A and AP2.

What is the A2A (Agent2Agent) protocol?

+
A2A is an open standard that lets AI agents built by different vendors discover each other, exchange messages and hand over tasks. It is governed by the Linux Foundation, and version 1.0 was released in March 2026. A business publishes an Agent Card that describes what it offers and how to reach it.

What is AP2 and how do AI agents pay for things?

+
AP2 (Agent Payments Protocol) is an open protocol, created by Google and donated to the FIDO Alliance in April 2026, that lets an AI agent make a payment on a customer's behalf. It uses signed mandates that record exactly what the customer authorised, so every agent purchase has verifiable proof.

What are UCP, MCP and Knowledge Catalog?

+
UCP (Universal Commerce Protocol) lets agents browse a catalogue, manage a cart and check out. MCP (Model Context Protocol) connects AI assistants to tools and data such as booking systems. Knowledge Catalog is Google Cloud's service, formerly Dataplex Universal Catalog, that gives AI agents governed business context about data. All three depend on accurate, structured business information, which is where we start.

Do I need to support every AI agent standard?

+
No. Most businesses should start with accurate structured data, clear prices and policies, and a simple way to book or buy. Standards such as A2A, AP2 and UCP are then added where they give a real benefit for your sector. Several of these standards are still changing, so we check the current specification before we build anything.

Can you guarantee more customers from AI agents?

+
No, and nobody honestly can yet. What we can do is measure how AI assistants find and describe your business before and after our work, and show you the difference. The free scanner at the top of this page gives you a starting score.
