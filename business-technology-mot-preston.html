Business Technology MOT for Preston and Lancashire Firms

The same UK-wide remote check, for firms close to home

Quick answer: A remote Business Technology MOT reviews agreed Windows computers, updates, protective settings, business apps and backup evidence. A person then writes the report. The introductory price is £99 for the first 10 eligible UK bookings, subject to availability. The standard Essential price is £149. Monitoring and repairs are separate. Firms in Preston, Penwortham and the wider Lancashire area use this same remote check. It is not a London-style rebuild.

Who it is for

Heimdell Tech Ai Ltd is registered at 11 Lower Croft, Preston, PR1 9DJ. The Business Technology MOT itself is a UK-wide remote review, not a visit with a ladder and a cable tester. That is why a firm in Preston, Penwortham or elsewhere in Lancashire can book the same check as a firm in another part of the United Kingdom.

It is meant as a first, bounded look at the computers you actually use: what is measured, what you tell us, and what we could not see. It is a reasonable starting point if you want priorities in writing before you spend money on maintenance, monitoring or any AI work. The full UK-wide description, prices and booking form are on the Business Technology MOT page. This page does not change those prices or those terms.

We do not publish a Preston case study. There is no client story on this page, because we do not have a public local result we are willing to present as proof. The local fact we can stand behind is the registered office, and the fact that the remote MOT is offered UK-wide.

What the remote MOT does not include

The published scope is specific. Physical cable wiremap testing, hardware repairs and whole-premises Wi-Fi surveys are not included in a remote review. The checks are not a penetration test and they are not a formal certification. Nothing on this page promises that a breach will be prevented.

Allow about 60 to 90 minutes for an Essential appointment. The timing and the access method are agreed first. If a computer is not in the agreed list, it is not part of the review. If a check needs someone on site with test equipment, that is a different job and it is quoted separately. Physical repair labour, where it is agreed and available, is from £65 an hour plus parts. It is not included in the remote inspection, and it is not a promise of a nationwide call-out.

What the report contains

The review looks at agreed Windows computers, performance, updates, available protective settings, business applications and backup evidence. A person reviews the report. Account security and business processes are checked with you, rather than guessed from a scan you never see.

Findings are separated into three kinds, and the report keeps them separate:

  • Measured results. What the agreed remote checks actually recorded on the computers in scope.
  • Customer answers. What you told us about how the business works, including account security and day-to-day processes.
  • Unverified areas. What was not tested. A remote review cannot pretend to have opened a cabinet, mapped a cable or surveyed Wi-Fi in every room.

That split matters. A recommendation that rests on a measurement is different from a recommendation that rests on an answer, and both are different from a gap we could not see. The report is there so you can choose what to improve, not so that a tool can declare the business “secure”.

Pilot availability and pricing are confirmed individually. These features are not generally available, and they are not a live product you can switch on from this page. Remote fixes and automation implementation are quoted on their own. They are not bundled into the £99 or £149 inspection.
