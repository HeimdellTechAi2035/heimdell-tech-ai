What Is Third Party Verification?

Third party verification is an independent confirmation process conducted by someone outside the original sales transaction. Its purpose is to verify that a customer understands what they are agreeing to, has provided informed consent, and is who they claim to be.

In telecom, TPV typically involves contacting the customer after a sale to confirm:

  • Their identity
  • The service or contract they agreed to
  • The price and contract terms
  • Their understanding of cancellation rights
  • Their authorisation for payment (such as Direct Debit)

The verification is recorded, timestamped, and stored as evidence. This creates an independent audit trail that protects the provider, the reseller, and the customer.

Why “third party”?

Unlike internal quality checks, third party verification is conducted by a separate entity with no stake in whether the sale completes. This independence is what gives TPV its legal and regulatory weight.

What Is Third Party Verification in Telecom?

In the telecom industry, third party verification serves a specific regulatory and commercial purpose.

UK telecom providers operate under Ofcom’s General Conditions, which include rules on:

  • Mis-selling prevention
  • Contract transparency
  • Customer consent documentation
  • Complaint handling procedures

Resellers, dealers, and sales agencies working with major providers (Virgin Media, O2, Sky, EE, and others) are contractually required to demonstrate compliant sales practices. TPV is the mechanism that proves compliance.

Disputed sales cost more than the immediate refund. They trigger clawbacks, damage provider relationships, and consume hours of administrative time.

What happens if a customer refuses to complete the verification call?

+
If a customer refuses or hangs up before verification is complete, the sale is logged as unverified rather than confirmed — it isn't recorded as a pass. In practice this means the agent or provider is alerted immediately so the sale can be followed up, re-attempted, or withdrawn before it's treated as a confirmed order.

What happens if the verification call goes unanswered or to voicemail?

+
An unanswered call doesn't count as a completed verification — the system logs it as a failed attempt and the call is retried within the verification window. If the customer still can't be reached, the sale stays unverified until contact is made, protecting you from treating an unconfirmed order as a completed sale.

Does the verification call replace the welcome call some providers require?

+
No — a verification call and a welcome call serve different purposes. The verification call independently confirms identity, contract terms, cooling-off rights and Direct Debit authority at the point of sale; a welcome call (where a provider requires one) is typically a later relationship-building touchpoint and isn't a substitute for independently verified consent evidence.

What's the difference between a verification call and a standard sales quality-assurance (QA) call?

+
A QA call is typically an internal spot-check on a small sample of sales calls to monitor agent performance, while a verification call is an independent check run on every relevant sale to confirm the customer's consent. QA reviews process; verification creates the evidence record that proves the sale itself was valid.

Can my commission still be clawed back even after a sale has passed verification?

+
Yes — verification protects you against disputes over consent, but it doesn't prevent every clawback. Commissions can still be reversed for reasons unrelated to consent, such as the customer cancelling within a provider's clawback window or failing to pay, since those are billing and churn outcomes rather than consent disputes.

Is third party verification a legal requirement for UK telecom sales, or just best practice?

+
In the UK, third party verification isn't a single named legal mandate the way it is in the US under FCC rules — it's a best-practice mechanism telecom resellers use to demonstrate compliance with Ofcom's General Conditions on consent, contract transparency and mis-selling. Many providers require it contractually even where it isn't a standalone statutory obligation.

Who actually makes the verification call — is it in-house staff or an independent third party?

+
The verification call is made by an independent agent who isn't involved in the original sale, not by the salesperson or their own team. That independence is what gives the verification evidentiary weight — a self-recorded confirmation by the seller carries far less credibility with providers and regulators.

Does the customer have to pay for the verification call?

+
No — the verification call is free for the customer to receive. The cost is covered by the reseller or provider running the verification, billed in credits (5 credits per phone verification) rather than passed on to the customer.

How does verification interact with the customer's 14-day cooling-off period?

+
Verification happens within 24 hours of the sale, well inside the standard 14-day cooling-off period for distance and off-premises telecom contracts. The verification call itself reconfirms the customer's cooling-off rights as part of the conversation, rather than starting or ending the cooling-off clock.
