The Problem: Compliance Gaps You Cannot See

Most UK service businesses operate with invisible regulatory exposure. Your sales team closes deals, commissions are paid, and everything appears to work — until a provider audit flags inconsistencies, a batch of clawbacks arrives, or a customer dispute escalates beyond your ability to defend it.

The underlying issue is rarely malicious non-compliance. It is structural: sales processes designed for speed and volume often lack the verification checkpoints, documentation trails and consent records that providers and regulators require. These gaps compound over time, creating liabilities that only surface during audits or disputes.

Commission clawbacks alone cost telecom resellers and sales agencies between 5% and 15% of gross commissions annually. Add provider audit failures, ICO complaints and potential enforcement action, and the total cost of unmanaged regulatory risk becomes substantial. Yet most agencies discover these gaps reactively — after the financial damage has occurred.

The core question: If a provider audited your last 100 sales tomorrow, how many would pass? If an ICO complaint landed on your desk, could you prove lawful processing? A Regulatory Risk Assessment answers these questions before stakeholders ask them.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a regulatory risk assessment for service businesses?

+
A regulatory risk assessment is a structured review of your sales process against the legal framework governing UK telecom reselling. It examines compliance with Ofcom General Conditions, Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013, and Payment Services Regulations 2017 to identify gaps that could trigger commission clawbacks, provider audits, or enforcement action.

What documents do I need to provide?

What happens after the assessment?

You receive a detailed risk matrix scoring each compliance area, a prioritised list of gaps requiring remediation, and a roadmap for implementing fixes. Many clients proceed to commission clawback audit or post-sale verification system deployment to address identified issues.

Is the assessment suitable for agencies selling for multiple providers?

+
Yes. The assessment covers cross-provider compliance requirements and identifies where your processes may satisfy one provider’s audit criteria but fail another’s. This is particularly valuable for agencies selling Virgin Media, O2, Sky, or BT products through multiple channel relationships.

What is PECR and how does it apply to telecom sales calls?

+
PECR (the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations 2003) is separate UK law that sits alongside UK GDPR and specifically governs marketing calls, texts, emails and cookies. For telecom sales teams it sets the rules on which numbers you can call, what consent you need, and what you must disclose during the call — breaching it carries its own ICO fines regardless of your GDPR compliance status.

What are the legal hours for telemarketing calls under PECR?

+
Unsolicited marketing calls are only permitted between 8am and 8pm on weekdays and 9am and 6pm on weekends and public holidays. Calling outside these windows, or calling a number registered with the Telephone Preference Service without prior consent, is a PECR breach the ICO actively enforces against.

What is the Telephone Preference Service (TPS) and do I have to screen against it before calling?

+
The TPS is the UK's official opt-out register for unsolicited sales and marketing calls, and PECR requires specific consent to call a TPS-registered number for marketing purposes. Skipping TPS screening is one of the most common breaches cited in real ICO enforcement notices against telemarketing firms.

What is the 'soft opt-in' and can telecom resellers use it to contact existing customers?

+
The soft opt-in lets you market similar products to existing customers without separate explicit consent, but only if their details were collected during an actual sale, they were given a clear opt-out at the time, and every message since has included one. It does not apply to prospects or bought-in contact lists — those still need explicit consent under PECR.

What is DUAA 2025 and when does it take effect?

+
The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 is the UK's reform of data protection law. It received royal assent on 19 June 2025 and is rolling out in phases, with most provisions taking effect from 5 February 2026 and final measures following in June 2026. It amends UK GDPR, the Data Protection Act 2018 and PECR rather than replacing them outright.

Does DUAA 2025 replace UK GDPR?

+
No — DUAA amends UK GDPR, the Data Protection Act 2018 and PECR with targeted changes rather than replacing them. The six lawful bases for processing, core data subject rights and the accountability principle all remain in place, so an existing UK GDPR compliance programme needs updating for DUAA, not rebuilding from scratch.

What is 'Recognised Legitimate Interest' under DUAA 2025?

+
It's a new, seventh lawful basis for processing personal data that DUAA adds to UK GDPR, covering specific purposes such as safeguarding vulnerable people, preventing crime or assisting public bodies, without the usual legitimate-interests balancing test. It's narrower than general legitimate interest and doesn't extend to ordinary telecom sales and marketing activity.

How much can the ICO actually fine a business for a PECR or UK GDPR breach?

+
For the most serious UK GDPR breaches the ICO can fine up to £17.5 million or 4% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher; a lower tier of up to £8.7 million or 2% applies to less severe breaches. PECR marketing breaches are enforced separately and have resulted in fines up to £500,000 against telemarketing firms.

What fines have real telemarketing companies received from the ICO recently?

+
Recent ICO enforcement includes a £90,000 fine against a compensation firm for unlawful marketing calls, £340,000 split across two companies for aggressive and unwanted calls, and a historic £500,000 fine against CRDNN for over 193 million automated nuisance calls. The ICO has issued more than £2.4 million in PECR fines since April 2022 alone.

Do the DUAA 2025 changes affect PECR marketing rules for telecom sales teams?

+
Yes — DUAA makes targeted amendments to PECR alongside UK GDPR, and ICO enforcement powers under both are being strengthened as part of the same Act, including the ability to compel witnesses and demand reports during investigations. A regulatory risk assessment needs to check PECR marketing practices against both the current rules and the DUAA changes taking effect through 2026.
